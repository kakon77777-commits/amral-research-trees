## Banwait–Huang, arXiv:2601.16044v3
Relevant:
- Proposition 2.9
- Remark 2.10
- Remark 2.11
- Theorem 2.14

Key verified fact:
semistability in the additive-twist branch is used only to produce the
ramified-prime condition; in the irreducible good-ordinary branch the same

## Fouquet–Wan, arXiv:2107.13726v3
Relevant:
- Theorem 1.7
- Corollary 1.10

Theorem 1.7 permits arbitrary reduction at p under residual hypotheses.
Corollary 1.10 gives the p-part BSD formula when the central L-value is
nonzero, with the modular-form period.

## Burungale–Castella–Skinner, arXiv:2405.00270v2
Relevant:
- Theorem 1.1.2
- Corollary 1.3.1

For p>3 of good ordinary reduction, the old ramified-prime hypothesis can be
removed under irreducibility and the condition (im).  The rank-0/1 p-part BSD
formula follows.

## Česnavičius–Neururer–Saha, JEMS 26 (2024), arXiv:1911.09446
Relevant introduction summary:
Edixhoven's theorem gives p not dividing the Manin constant for p>=11 at an
additive prime unless the reduction is additive potentially ordinary of
Kodaira type II, III, or IV.  The paper also proves strong general
divisibility results for Manin constants and modular degrees.
